# Math question samples

One question per template from each pilot topic, taken from the generated banks. ✔ marks the correct option. Each wrong option shows the mistake it represents.

## Fractions

### Basic

**frac-b-001** (simplest_form). Express 36/48 in its simplest form.

- A. 18/24: *stopped_before_simplest* (Divided by 2, a common factor, but not by the HCF 12.)
- B. 3/4 ✔
- C. 1/16: *divided_numerator_only* (Divided only the numerator by 12.)
- D. 4/3: *inverted_fraction* (Turned the fraction upside down.)

Solution: HCF of 36 and 48 is 12. → 36 ÷ 12 = 3, 48 ÷ 12 = 4. → 36/48 = 3/4

**frac-b-002** (add_unlike). Find the value of 1/2 + 9/11. Give your answer in its simplest form.

- A. 10/11: *renamed_only_first_fraction* (Changed 1/2 to 11/22 but did not change the numerator 9 of 9/11.)
- B. 10/13: *added_numerators_and_denominators* (Added the tops and added the bottoms.)
- C. 2 7/22: *slip_converting_to_mixed_number* (Made a slip changing the improper fraction to a mixed number.)
- D. 1 7/22 ✔

Solution: LCM of 2 and 11 is 22. → 1/2 = 11/22, 9/11 = 18/22. → 11/22 + 18/22 = 29/22 = 1 7/22

**frac-b-003** (frac_times_whole). Find the value of 3/8 × 7.

- A. 3 5/8: *slip_converting_to_mixed_number* (Made a slip changing the improper fraction to a mixed number.)
- B. 1 1/4: *added_whole_to_numerator* (Added 7 to the numerator.)
- C. 3/56: *multiplied_denominator* (Multiplied the denominator by 7 instead of the numerator.)
- D. 2 5/8 ✔

Solution: 3/8 × 7 = 3 × 7 / 8 = 21/8 → = 2 5/8

**frac-b-004** (frac_div_whole). Find the value of 3/7 ÷ 7.

- A. 16 1/3: *inverted_answer* (Turned the answer upside down.)
- B. 3/49 ✔
- C. 4/49: *careless_slip_in_numerator* (Careless slip working out the numerator.)
- D. 3: *multiplied_instead_of_dividing* (Multiplied by 7 instead of dividing.)

Solution: Dividing by 7 is the same as multiplying by 1/7. → 3/7 × 1/7 = 3/49 → = 3/49

**frac-b-005** (whole_div_frac). Find the value of 9 ÷ 3/4.

- A. 6 3/4: *did_not_invert_divisor* (Multiplied by 3/4 instead of 4/3.)
- B. 1/12: *inverted_answer* (Turned the answer upside down.)
- C. 12 ✔
- D. 36: *ignored_numerator* (Multiplied by 4 but forgot to divide by 3.)

Solution: Dividing by 3/4 is the same as multiplying by 4/3. → 9 × 4/3 = 36/3 → = 12

**frac-b-006** (fraction_of_quantity). What is 2/7 of $56?

- A. $8: *found_one_part_only* (Divided by 7 but did not multiply by 2.)
- B. $24: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- C. $16 ✔
- D. $40: *found_the_other_part* (Found the remaining 5/7 instead.)

Solution: 1/7 of 56 = 56 ÷ 7 = 8 → 2/7 of 56 = 2 × 8 = 16

**frac-b-007** (fraction_to_decimal). Express 7/12 as a decimal, correct to 2 decimal places.

- A. 0.59: *rounding_slip* (Rounded up when the next digit was less than 5.)
- B. 0.58 ✔
- C. 1.71: *divided_the_wrong_way* (Worked out 12 ÷ 7 instead of 7 ÷ 12.)
- D. 0.6: *rounded_to_1_decimal_place* (Rounded to 1 decimal place instead of 2.)

Solution: 7/12 = 7 ÷ 12 = 0.5833… → The third decimal place is 3, so round down. → Answer: 0.58

**frac-b-008** (share_remainder). Arjun used 1/3 of a 5 m long rope. The rest of the rope was cut into 6 equal pieces. How long is each piece?

- A. 5/6 m: *ignored_the_used_part* (Shared the whole rope.)
- B. 5/18 m: *shared_the_used_part* (Shared the 1/3 that was used instead of the rest.)
- C. 2/3 m: *careless_slip_in_numerator* (Careless slip working out the numerator.)
- D. 5/9 m ✔

Solution: Rest = (1 − 1/3) × 5 m = 3 1/3 m → Each piece = 3 1/3 ÷ 6 = 5/9 m

### Intermediate

**frac-i-001** (mixed_subtract_borrow). A ribbon is 9 1/4 m long. Kumar cuts off 2 1/3 m. How long is the ribbon that is left?

- A. 7 1/12 m: *subtracted_smaller_fraction_from_larger* (Did 1/3 − 1/4 because 1/4 is smaller, instead of renaming 1 whole.)
- B. 7 11/12 m: *forgot_to_take_away_the_borrowed_whole* (Renamed 1 whole as a fraction but did not reduce the whole number by 1.)
- C. 6 11/12 m ✔
- D. 7 m: *subtracted_numerators_and_denominators* (Subtracted tops and bottoms separately.)

Solution: 9 1/4 = 8 15/12 (rename 1 whole) → 2 1/3 = 2 4/12 → 8 15/12 − 2 4/12 = 6 11/12 m

**frac-i-002** (remainder_left). Siti had $75. Siti spent 1/3 of it on a bag and 4/5 of the remainder on a book. How much money did Siti have left?

- A. $40: *gave_cost_of_book* (Gave the cost of the book instead of the money left.)
- B. $10 ✔
- C. $50: *stopped_after_first_step* (Stopped after paying for the bag.)
- D. $25: *gave_cost_of_bag* (Gave the cost of the bag.)

Solution: Bag: 1/3 × $75 = $25 → Remainder: $75 − $25 = $50 → Book: 4/5 × $50 = $40 → Left: $50 − $40 = $10

**frac-i-003** (more_boys_than_girls). 5/9 of the pupils in a hall are boys. There are 7 more boys than girls. How many pupils are there in the hall?

- A. 63 pupils ✔
- B. 35 pupils: *gave_number_of_boys* (Found the number of boys only.)
- C. 28 pupils: *gave_number_of_girls* (Found the number of girls only.)
- D. 70 pupils: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Girls = 1 − 5/9 = 4/9 → Difference = 5/9 − 4/9 = 1/9 → 7 pupils → 1/9 → 7 ÷ 1 = 7 → Total = 9 × 7 = 63

**frac-i-004** (share_into_bags). 8/9 kg of flour is packed equally into 8 bags. What is the mass of flour in each bag?

- A. 9 kg: *divided_bags_by_flour* (Worked out 8 ÷ 8/9 instead of 8/9 ÷ 8.)
- B. 1/9 kg ✔
- C. 2/9 kg: *careless_slip_in_numerator* (Careless slip working out the numerator.)
- D. 7 1/9 kg: *multiplied_instead_of_dividing* (Multiplied by 8 instead of dividing.)

Solution: 8/9 ÷ 8 = 8/9 × 1/8 → = 1/9 kg

**frac-i-005** (how_many_cups). A jug holds 4 litres of juice. How many cups of 2/9 litre can be filled completely?

- A. 36 cups: *ignored_numerator* (Multiplied by 9 but did not divide by 2.)
- B. 2 cups: *ignored_denominator* (Divided by 2 but did not multiply by 9.)
- C. 18 cups ✔
- D. 38 cups: *added_instead_of_divided* (Mixed up the steps.)

Solution: 4 ÷ 2/9 = 4 × 9/2 → = 36/2 = 18

**frac-i-006** (fraction_of_remainder). 1/3 of a cake was left after a party. Shu Fen ate 1/2 of the cake that was left. What fraction of the whole cake did Shu Fen eat?

- A. 1/6 ✔
- B. 1/2: *fraction_of_remainder_as_fraction_of_whole* (Gave 1/2, which is a fraction of the leftover only.)
- C. 1/3: *careless_slip_in_numerator* (Careless slip working out the numerator.)
- D. 2/5: *added_numerators_and_denominators* (Added tops and bottoms.)

Solution: 1/2 of 1/3 = 1/2 × 1/3 → = 1/6

**frac-i-007** (fixed_then_fraction). Aisha had some money. Aisha spent $10 on a book and 1/3 of the remaining money on a bag. Aisha had $20 left. How much money did Aisha have at first?

- A. $30: *forgot_to_add_back_fixed_amount* (Worked back to $30 but did not add the $10 for the book.)
- B. $40 ✔
- C. $45: *added_fixed_amount_before_working_back* (Added $10 before undoing the 1/3.)
- D. $50: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Money left = 2/3 of the remainder → $20 → Remainder = $20 ÷ 2 × 3 = $30 → At first = $30 + $10 = $40

### Competent

**frac-c-001** (remainder_backwards). Jun Wei spent 3/8 of the money in a wallet on a pair of shoes and 2/5 of the remaining money on a shirt. Jun Wei had $120 left. How much money was in the wallet at first?

- A. $320 ✔
- B. $360: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- C. $120: *gave_cost_of_shoes* (Gave the cost of the shoes.)
- D. $200: *worked_back_only_one_step* (Found the money left after the shoes only.)

Solution: Money left = 3/5 of the remainder → $120 → Remainder = $120 ÷ 3 × 5 = $200 → Remainder = 5/8 of the money → $200 → At first = $200 ÷ 5 × 8 = $320

**frac-c-002** (book_more_than_pen). Mei Ling spent 2/5 of a sum of money on a book and 3/5 of the remainder on a pen. The book cost $5 more than the pen. How much money did Mei Ling have at first?

- A. $50: *gave_cost_of_book* (Gave the cost of the book.)
- B. $75: *gave_remainder_after_book* (Gave the money left after buying the book.)
- C. $125 ✔
- D. $130: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Pen = 3/5 × 3/5 = 9/25 of the money → Book − pen = 2/5 − 9/25 = 1/25 of the money → 1/25 → $5 → Money at first = $5 ÷ 1/25 = $125

**frac-c-003** (equal_fractions). 3/5 of Farid's stickers is equal to 4/5 of Aisha's stickers. Farid has 10 more stickers than Aisha. How many stickers do they have altogether?

- A. 80 stickers: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- B. 30 stickers: *gave_second_person_only* (Gave Aisha's stickers only.)
- C. 40 stickers: *gave_first_person_only* (Gave Farid's stickers only.)
- D. 70 stickers ✔

Solution: Make the numerators the same: 3/5 of Farid = 4/5 of Aisha → Farid : Aisha = 20 : 15 = 4 : 3 → 1 units → 10, so 1 unit → 10 → Altogether = 7 × 10 = 70

**frac-c-004** (glasses_in_groups). 3/4 of the pupils in a school hall are boys. 1/3 of the boys and 2/5 of the girls wear glasses. 42 pupils wear glasses. How many pupils are in the hall?

- A. 120 pupils ✔
- B. 78 pupils: *gave_pupils_without_glasses* (Gave the number of pupils without glasses.)
- C. 90 pupils: *gave_number_of_boys* (Gave the number of boys.)
- D. 150 pupils: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Boys with glasses = 1/3 × 3/4 = 1/4 of the pupils → Girls with glasses = 2/5 × 1/4 = 1/10 of the pupils → Glasses = 7/20 of the pupils → 42 → Pupils = 42 ÷ 7/20 = 120

**frac-c-005** (constant_part_new_fraction). In a jar, 5/8 of the beads are red and the rest are blue. After 60 blue beads are added, the red beads make up 1/4 of all the beads. How many beads were in the jar at first?

- A. 40 beads ✔
- B. 160 beads: *treated_change_in_fraction_as_added_beads* (Took 60 as (5/8 − 1/4) of the beads, but the whole changed.)
- C. 45 beads: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- D. 100 beads: *gave_total_after* (Gave the number of beads after the blue beads were added.)

Solution: The red beads do not change. Make red the same number of units: LCM of 5 and 1 is 5 → Before: red 5 units of 8; after: red 5 units of 20 → 12 units → 60, so 1 unit = 5 → At first = 8 × 5 = 40

**frac-c-006** (unequal_wholes_same_remainder). Aisha had $32 more than Mei Ling. Aisha spent 1/2 of Aisha's money and Mei Ling spent 1/4 of Mei Ling's money. They then had the same amount of money left. How much money did they have altogether at first?

- A. $160 ✔
- B. $64: *gave_second_person_only* (Gave Mei Ling's money at first only.)
- C. $96: *gave_first_person_only* (Gave Aisha's money at first only.)
- D. $192: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Left: 1/2 of Aisha's = 3/4 of Mei Ling's money → Aisha : Mei Ling = 3/4 : 1/2 = 3 : 2 → Difference 1 units → $32, so 1 unit = $32 → Altogether = (3 + 2) × $32 = $160

## Ratio

### Basic

**ratio-b-001** (simplest_ratio). Express 18 : 81 in its simplest form.

- A. 2 : 27: *divided_terms_by_different_numbers* (Divided the last term by 3 instead of 9.)
- B. 2 : 9 ✔
- C. 6 : 27: *stopped_before_simplest* (Divided by 3 but not by the HCF 9.)
- D. 9 : 2: *reversed_order* (Wrote the terms in the wrong order.)

Solution: HCF of 18, 81 is 9. → Divide each term by 9: 2 : 9

**ratio-b-002** (divide_in_ratio). Nurul and Aisha share $102 in the ratio 1 : 5. How much does the person with the larger share get?

- A. $102: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- B. $85 ✔
- C. $68: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too few.)
- D. $17: *found_one_unit_only* (Found 1 unit ($102 ÷ 6) but did not multiply.)

Solution: Total units = 1 + 5 = 6 → 1 unit = $102 ÷ 6 = $17 → Larger share = 5 × $17 = $85

**ratio-b-003** (missing_term). Find the missing number: 9 : 5 = 36 : ?

- A. 20 ✔
- B. 15: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too few.)
- C. 25: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- D. 32: *added_the_difference* (Added 27 to 5 instead of multiplying by 4.)

Solution: 36 = 9 × 4 → Missing number = 5 × 4 = 20

**ratio-b-004** (ratio_with_units). Find the ratio of 30 minutes to 4 hours. Give your answer in its simplest form.

- A. 1 : 8 ✔
- B. 8 : 1: *reversed_order* (Wrote the terms in the wrong order.)
- C. 3 : 40: *used_100_minutes_in_an_hour* (Used 100 minutes in an hour.)
- D. 15 : 2: *forgot_to_convert_units* (Compared 30 with 4 without changing hours to minutes.)

Solution: 4 h = 240 min → 30 : 240 = 1 : 8

**ratio-b-005** (ratio_to_fraction). The ratio of blue beads to red beads in a box is 9 : 4. What fraction of the beads are blue beads?

- A. 4/13: *found_fraction_of_other_colour* (Found the fraction of red beads.)
- B. 10/13: *careless_slip_in_numerator* (Careless slip working out the numerator.)
- C. 9/4: *used_other_part_as_whole* (Wrote 9/4, comparing with red beads instead of all the beads.)
- D. 9/13 ✔

Solution: Total units = 9 + 4 = 13 → Fraction of blue beads = 9/13

**ratio-b-006** (fraction_to_ratio). The number of boys in a club is 1/2 of the number of girls. What is the ratio of the number of boys to the total number of children?

- A. 2 : 3: *gave_girls_to_total* (Gave girls : total.)
- B. 1 : 1: *subtracted_instead_of_added* (Used 2 − 1 for the total.)
- C. 1 : 3 ✔
- D. 1 : 2: *gave_boys_to_girls* (Gave boys : girls, not boys : total.)

Solution: Boys : girls = 1 : 2 → Total = 1 + 2 = 3 units → Boys : total = 1 : 3

### Intermediate

**ratio-i-001** (ratio_difference_to_total). The ratio of Ali's marbles to Siti's marbles is 6 : 5. Ali has 3 more marbles than Siti. How many marbles do they have altogether?

- A. 33 marbles ✔
- B. 36 marbles: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- C. 15 marbles: *gave_second_person_only* (Gave Siti's marbles only.)
- D. 18 marbles: *gave_first_person_only* (Gave Ali's marbles only.)

Solution: Difference = 6 − 5 = 1 units → 3 → 1 unit = 3 ÷ 1 = 3 → Altogether = 11 × 3 = 33

**ratio-i-002** (combine_ratios). The ratio of A to B is 3 : 7. The ratio of B to C is 4 : 7. Find the ratio of A to B to C.

- A. 3 : 7 : 7: *joined_without_matching_B* (Joined the ratios as 3 : 7 : 7 without making B the same.)
- B. 49 : 28 : 12: *reversed_order* (Wrote the ratio backwards.)
- C. 12 : 28 : 7: *scaled_only_first_ratio* (Changed the first ratio but not the second.)
- D. 12 : 28 : 49 ✔

Solution: Make B the same: LCM of 7 and 4 is 28 → A : B = 12 : 28, B : C = 28 : 49 → A : B : C = 12 : 28 : 49

**ratio-i-003** (part_to_total). The ratio of boys to girls in a choir is 8 : 3. There are 21 girls. How many children are in the choir?

- A. 77 children ✔
- B. 56 children: *gave_number_of_boys* (Gave the number of boys.)
- C. 231 children: *did_not_find_one_unit* (Multiplied 21 by 11 without finding 1 unit.)
- D. 84 children: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: 3 units → 21 → 1 unit = 21 ÷ 3 = 7 → Total = 11 × 7 = 77

**ratio-i-004** (three_way_share). $120 is shared among three friends in the ratio 1 : 2 : 9. How much more does the person with the largest share get than the person with the smallest share?

- A. $10: *found_one_unit_only* (Found 1 unit only.)
- B. $90: *gave_largest_share* (Gave the largest share instead of the difference.)
- C. $70: *compared_wrong_shares* (Compared the largest with the middle share.)
- D. $80 ✔

Solution: Total units = 12 → 1 unit = $120 ÷ 12 = $10 → Difference = (9 − 1) × $10 = $80

**ratio-i-005** (multiple_and_ratio). Hui Min, Siti and Ali share $77. Hui Min gets 2 times as much as Siti. The ratio of Siti's share to Ali's share is 3 : 2. How much does Ali get?

- A. $42: *gave_first_person* (Gave Hui Min's share.)
- B. $14 ✔
- C. $22: *used_multiple_as_ratio_term* (Took Hui Min : Siti : Ali as 2 : 3 : 2 without matching Siti.)
- D. $21: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)

Solution: Siti : Ali = 3 : 2, so Hui Min = 2 × 3 = 6 units → Hui Min : Siti : Ali = 6 : 3 : 2 → 1 unit = $77 ÷ 11 = $7 → Ali = 2 × $7 = $14

### Competent

**ratio-c-001** (transfer_total_unchanged). The ratio of Priya's cards to Wei Jie's cards was 2 : 7. After Priya gave 3 cards to Wei Jie, the ratio became 1 : 4. How many cards did Priya have at first?

- A. 27 cards: *gave_amount_after_transfer* (Gave Priya's cards after giving some away.)
- B. 105 cards: *gave_other_person* (Gave Wei Jie's cards at first.)
- C. 33 cards: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- D. 30 cards ✔

Solution: The total does not change. Make the totals equal: 9 and 5 → 45 units → Before 10 : 35, after 9 : 36 → Priya lost 1 units → 3 cards, so 1 unit = 3 → Priya at first = 10 × 3 = 30

**ratio-c-002** (one_quantity_unchanged). The ratio of red balloons to blue balloons at a party was 7 : 5. After 16 more red balloons were added, the ratio became 5 : 3. How many balloons were there at first?

- A. 148 balloons: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- B. 84 balloons: *gave_red_only* (Gave the red balloons at first only.)
- C. 144 balloons ✔
- D. 160 balloons: *gave_total_after* (Gave the total after the red balloons were added.)

Solution: Blue does not change. Make blue the same: 5 and 3 → 15 units → Before 21 : 15, after 25 : 15 → Red increased by 4 units → 16, so 1 unit = 4 → At first = (21 + 15) × 4 = 144

**ratio-c-003** (ages_constant_difference). The ratio of Nurul's age to Hui Min's age is 4 : 1. In 3 years' time, the ratio will be 3 : 1. How old is Nurul now?

- A. 6 years old: *gave_other_person* (Gave Hui Min's age.)
- B. 24 years old ✔
- C. 27 years old: *gave_age_in_future* (Gave Nurul's age in 3 years' time.)
- D. 12 years old: *compared_terms_without_matching_difference* (Compared the ratios without keeping the age difference the same.)

Solution: The age difference does not change. Make it the same: 3 and 2 → 6 units → Now 8 : 2, later 9 : 3 → 1 units → 3 years, so 1 unit = 3 → Nurul now = 8 × 3 = 24

**ratio-c-004** (give_fraction_new_ratio). The ratio of Kumar's stickers to Aisha's stickers is 7 : 6. Kumar gives 2/5 of Kumar's stickers to Aisha. What is the new ratio of Kumar's stickers to Aisha's stickers?

- A. 9 : 56: *took_fraction_of_total* (Took 2/5 of all the stickers.)
- B. 5 : 8: *moved_numerator_as_units* (Moved 2 units instead of 2/5 of Kumar's share.)
- C. 21 : 44 ✔
- D. 7 : 10: *forgot_to_add_to_other_person* (Took the stickers from Kumar but did not add them to Aisha.)

Solution: Let Kumar = 35 units and Aisha = 30 units (multiply by 5) → Kumar gives 2/5 × 35 = 14 units → New: 21 : 44 = 21 : 44

**ratio-c-005** (square_sides_area). The lengths of the sides of two squares are in the ratio 1 : 4. The difference between their areas is 240 cm². What is the area of the bigger square?

- A. 16 cm²: *gave_smaller_square* (Gave the area of the smaller square.)
- B. 256 cm² ✔
- C. 272 cm²: *miscounted_units* (Counted one unit too many.)
- D. 320 cm²: *used_side_ratio_for_areas* (Used 1 : 4 for the areas instead of 1 : 16.)

Solution: Areas are in the ratio 1² : 4² = 1 : 16 → Difference = 15 units → 240 cm² → 1 unit = 16 cm² → Bigger square = 16 × 16 = 256 cm²
